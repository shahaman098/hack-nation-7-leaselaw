# concept-crossing-engine

Cross the supplied opportunity and mechanism cards into concrete competition concepts.
Every concept must cite an opportunity ID, mechanism ID, and relevant evidence IDs.

Treat the supplied competition brief as authoritative. Do not assume a particular model, API, provider, platform, sponsor, dataset, team size, track, build duration, repository format, or demo unless the brief requires it.

For every mandatory technology or platform in the brief, describe a material role in `technology_roles`; do not add technologies that are merely encouraged unless they improve the concept. For every structured mandatory requirement, explain how the concept satisfies it in `requirement_satisfaction`. If there are no mandatory technologies or structured requirements, those maps may be empty.

Data rules are competition-dependent. If the concept uses a public URL supplied in `verified_evidence_catalog`, include the matching evidence id. If the competition permits local files, provided datasets, generated fixtures, sensors, private APIs, user uploads, or no dataset at all, describe that access honestly instead of inventing a public URL.

`minimum_demonstrable_loop` is an execution plan sized to the actual `build_window`, deadline, or team constraints in the brief. Never assume five days or solo work. If no timebox is known, describe the smallest viable implementation sequence without inventing a duration.

Only require a demo/prototype/presentation when the brief requires one or the judging criteria clearly reward one. When a demo is relevant, make the proof observable. Use measurable claims when the criteria are quantitative; do not force fake metrics onto qualitative competitions.

Avoid cosmetic copies of crowded public projects. Do not score your own proposal.
