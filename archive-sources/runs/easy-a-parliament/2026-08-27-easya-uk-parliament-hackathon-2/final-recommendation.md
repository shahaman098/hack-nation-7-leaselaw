# Final recommendation: UK Parliament Hackathon with EasyA

## Primary concept
**Working title:** Casework Correction Receipt
**Internal id:** `mut-r1-01-casework-correction-receipt`
**Primary user:** MP constituency office staff handling resident casework escalations before a short parliamentary technology demo
**Painful workflow:** A resident or staffer spots a wrong eligibility status, missed evidence item, or misclassified urgency in a casework queue, but the correction path is usually buried in email threads, spreadsheets, and manual notes. The office needs a way to verify the proposed correction against available evidence and write the corrected state back into the workflow without asking a technical analyst to rebuild the queue.
**Imported mechanism:** m-001 verifiable-provenance-ledger adapted from supply-chain audit to resident casework correction events. _(from forensic accounting)_
**Data sources:** Generated fictional constituency case records with status, owner, deadline, evidence references, and current workflow step, Generated fictional evidence notes or attachments represented by local text and hashes, User-entered correction request during the demo, Signed correction events and verifier outputs generated by the prototype
**Core computation:** An AI verifier reads a proposed correction, compares it with the resident's fictional evidence bundle and existing case record, classifies whether the correction is supported, partially supported, or unsupported, and generates a structured correction patch. The patch is written as a new signed event in an append-only cryptographic ledger and updates the visible case status only when the support threshold and human confirmation are satisfied.
**Last-mile action:** The staffer applies a verified correction directly to the casework queue: the affected field changes, the next owner is notified, and a correction receipt records the before value, after value, supporting evidence hashes, verifier rationale, timestamp, and reviewer confirmation.
**Visible transformation:** A disputed case record goes from an ambiguous complaint in an inbox to a corrected workflow item with a visible receipt showing exactly what changed, why it was accepted, and which evidence supports the change.
**Killer demo:** Load a fictional case marked 'standard priority' despite evidence of an imminent housing deadline. A judge submits a correction request, the AI cites the matching evidence note, the ledger records the before/after status change, and the queue immediately moves the case to urgent review with a QR-verifiable correction receipt.
**Hard-to-fake advantage:** A generic casework chatbot can suggest a correction, but this prototype must produce a structured patch, require human confirmation, update the workflow state, and preserve tamper-evident proof of the evidence and reviewer action.
**Sponsor dependency:** Low. EasyA provides the parliamentary audience and Web3 context, but the prototype does not depend on a sponsor SDK, sponsor dataset, or specific blockchain.

## Primary failure risk
Stronger event fit than a generic assistant because the state change plus receipt is observable. Risk remains around overclaiming verification: the demo must say the AI supports review, not proves resident truth. No real casework data should be used.

- Fatal flaws: ["Hidden structural twin of the crowded 'policy simulator / AI impact dashboard' archetype: natural-language rule in, before/after charts out, plain-English brief. Without a uniquely constrained domain or proprietary workflow integration, judges may read it as a polished civic-tech dashboard rather than a new mechanism.", "The core depends on fragile live LLM parsing. If the parser turns a parliamentarian's rule into the wrong predicates, the demo either silently misleads or exposes that the product cannot safely handle the very ambiguity it claims to solve.", 'ChatGPT-replaceable surface is high. A generic chatbot plus a spreadsheet can already summarize a proposed rule, list assumptions, and produce caveats. The proposed hard-to-fake advantage only survives if the executable predicates and attribution are visibly rigorous, not just JSON-shaped LLM output.', 'The data claims are inherently unverifiable because the baseline dataset is synthetic. The demo can show internal consistency, but it cannot prove real-world policy consequence validity, constituency relevance, distributional fairness, or administrative feasibility.', 'Demo theatre risk is severe: changing one sentence and watching charts move is visually convincing even if the predicates, uncertainty bands, and segment deltas are hand-tuned around a small fixture. EasyA/Web3 context is mostly audience decoration; there is no sponsor SDK, blockchain primitive, or venue-specific capability materially improving the system. The technology role text even treats the in-person venue as if it were part of the core mechanism, which is a credibility smell.', 'Uncertainty ranges may look scientific without a defensible statistical model. On a generated fixture, uncertainty is likely arbitrary sensitivity analysis rather than calibrated uncertainty, which creates overclaim risk in exactly the institutional setting the concept targets.']
- Survive if: ['Narrow the demo to one concrete fictional policy domain with a published schema, deterministic rule DSL, and 3-5 rule types the system can parse reliably.', 'Make the LLM produce a constrained intermediate representation that users can edit before simulation, with validation failures shown clearly instead of forcing every natural-language input through.', "Replace broad 'policy consequence' claims with 'scenario consistency check on a declared synthetic baseline' and label uncertainty as sensitivity bounds, not prediction.", 'Add adversarial demo cases where unsupported or ambiguous rule text is rejected, so the product proves restraint rather than only producing attractive output.', 'Show an audit trail from source fields to predicates to affected records to aggregate deltas, with at least one judge-edited rule that was not pre-scripted.', 'Tie the pitch to a real last-mile parliamentary workflow, such as scrutiny questions or committee brief preparation, rather than a generic impact dashboard.']
